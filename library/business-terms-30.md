# Thirty business terms

Source: @marketingideas365. Thirty definitions. The caption’s point is that the numbers and the language decide whether a company works, not the idea alone.

1. **MVP — minimum viable product.** The most basic working version, made to test an idea, gather feedback, and see if demand is real.
2. **PMF — product-market fit.** The product meets a real market need and draws customers who keep wanting to use or buy it.
3. **CAC — customer acquisition cost.** The average spent on marketing and sales to gain one new customer.
4. **LTV — lifetime value.** The estimated total revenue or profit a customer brings over the relationship. Which of those it is depends on how the metric is calculated.
5. **Runway.** How long the company can keep operating on the cash it has before it needs more funding or reaches positive cash flow.
6. **Burn rate.** How fast the business spends its available cash, usually measured monthly. Net burn is the cash lost after incoming cash is counted.
7. **ARR — annual recurring revenue.** The annualized value of predictable recurring revenue from subscriptions or ongoing contracts.
8. **MRR — monthly recurring revenue.** The predictable revenue generated each month from active subscriptions or other recurring payments.
9. **Churn rate.** The percentage of customers who cancel, leave, or stop paying during a period.
10. **Retention rate.** The percentage of customers who keep using or buying over a period.
11. **EBITDA.** Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization. Used to judge operating performance.
12. **Gross margin.** The percentage of revenue left after the direct costs of producing the goods or delivering the services.
13. **TAM — total addressable market.** The total potential revenue if the company could serve every customer in its defined market.
14. **SAM — serviceable available market.** The part of that total the business can serve with its products, model, and geographic reach.
15. **SOM — serviceable obtainable market.** The realistic share of the serviceable market the company expects to capture, given competition, resources, and market conditions.
16. **Cap table — capitalization table.** The document of who owns the company: shareholders, investors, equity percentages, and classes of securities.
17. **Equity.** An ownership stake, typically shares or other ownership interests.
18. **Vesting.** Employees, founders, or other stakeholders earn ownership rights to shares or options over a set period, or after meeting conditions.
19. **ESOP — employee stock ownership plan.** The slide’s definition: a qualified employee benefit plan that gives employees an ownership interest, generally through shares held in a trust.
20. **Dilution.** An existing shareholder’s ownership percentage falls when the company issues more shares or other securities and the total share count rises.
21. **Angel investor.** A person who invests their own money in startups or early-stage companies, often for an equity stake.
22. **VC — venture capital.** Funding for startups and growing businesses with large growth potential, typically through professionally managed funds.
23. **Seed round.** An early raise used to develop the product, test demand, hire the first employees, and set up operations.
24. **Series A.** A round for startups that have shown early traction and want capital to strengthen the model and grow faster.
25. **Series B.** A later round aimed at expanding operations, growing teams, reaching more of the market, and scaling a model that is already working.
26. **Series C.** A later-stage round often used for major expansion, new markets, more products, or acquisitions.
27. **Valuation.** An estimate of what the company is worth, from revenue, profitability, growth potential, assets, and market conditions.
28. **Pre-money valuation.** The estimated value immediately before new investment comes in.
29. **Post-money valuation.** The estimated value immediately after a round, generally the pre-money value plus the new investment.
30. **Term sheet.** A document of the proposed investment terms: valuation, amount, ownership, investor rights, and other conditions.
