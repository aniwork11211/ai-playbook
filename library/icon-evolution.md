# The evolution of icons

Source: @figma.expert. Icon Evolution Series, slides 1 through 7. The cover says 120 icons, 7 eras, one visual evolution. Each era slide has six styles. The three-word line next to a style is the slide’s own label.

Use this file when the work is choosing an icon style. Open it from `C:\Users\ANISH\Documents\ai-playbook\LIBRARY.md` and use only the era the task needs.

The cover’s timeline, left to right: Early GUI (1990s), Skeuomorphism (2000s), Flat Design (2010s), Material Style (mid 2010s), Neumorphism (2020), Glassmorphism (2022), Modern Adaptive (2024+). The date on each slide below is the badge printed on that slide.

## Early GUI to skeuomorphism

Badge: 1990s–2000s. Icons started as simple pixels and became detailed drawings of physical objects.

1. Pixel icons (1990s). Simple. Functional. Iconic.
2. Early system icons. Minimal shapes. Better clarity.
3. Xerox style (1970s–1990s). The roots of modern UI.
4. Skeuomorphic icons (2000s). Real-world inspiration.
5. Glossy and textured. Depth, shine, and realism.
6. Real-world metaphors. Icons people instantly understand.

## Flat design

Badge: 2010s–early 2010s. Flat design dropped extra detail for simplicity, clarity, and recognition. Icons got smaller, more versatile, and easier to scale.

1. Outline icons. Minimal. Timeless. Versatile.
2. Filled icons. Bold. Clear. Stronger hierarchy.
3. Rounded icons. Friendly. Modern. Approachable.
4. Monoline icons. Consistent. Balanced. Elegant.
5. Colored flat icons. Simple colors. Stronger meaning.
6. Minimal icons. Less detail. More impact.

## Material and modern systems

Badge: mid 2010s–late 2010s. Material Design and design systems made icons consistent, grid-based, and built for more than one platform, size, and state.

1. Material outlined. Clean. Minimal. Scalable.
2. Material filled. Bold. Clear. Better visibility.
3. Material rounded. Softer. Friendlier. Modern feel.
4. Material sharp. Geometric. Precise. Modern.
5. Two-tone icons. More depth. Better hierarchy.
6. Adaptive and dynamic. Flexible. Multi-platform. Future-ready.

## Neumorphism to glassmorphism

Badge: 2020–2022. Depth, light, shadow, blur, and translucent materials.

1. Neumorphic icons. Soft. Subtle. Elevated.
2. Soft UI / clay icons. Friendly. Tactile. Modern.
3. Glassmorphism icons. Translucent. Elegant. Futuristic.
4. Gradient icons. Vibrant. Expressive. Eye-catching.
5. 3D / realistic icons. Real-world feel. Depth. Premium.
6. Blur and glow icons. Modern. Ambient. Immersive.

## Apple and adaptive icon systems

Badge: 2020–2024+. Apple’s SF Symbols and adaptive systems: clarity, consistency, and flexibility, with multiple weights, styles, and rendering modes.

1. SF Symbols, outline. Clean lines. Maximum clarity.
2. SF Symbols, filled. Bold. Better visibility.
3. Hierarchical icons. Adaptive to context. Multiple levels.
4. Palette icons. Expressive. Layered colors.
5. Multicolor icons. Vibrant. Modern. Lively.
6. Adaptive / variable icons. Flexible. Responsive. Future-ready.

## The next era

Badge: 2024 and beyond. The slide’s claim is icons that mix 3D, motion, AI, and adaptive design.

1. 3D icons. More depth. More realism.
2. Gradient icons. Vibrant. Modern. Eye-catching.
3. Animated icons (motion). Alive. Interactive. Engaging.
4. AI-generated / intelligent icons. Dynamic. Context-aware. Smarter.
5. Experimental and creative icons. Bold ideas. New possibilities.
6. Adaptive / responsive icons. One icon. Every screen.
